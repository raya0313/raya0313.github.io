TVCG / ISMAR 2026 - Accepted

Effects of Spatial Constraint on Bare-Hand Logographic Character Handwriting Performance in Virtual Reality Studies how spatial constraints shape bare-hand logographic character handwriting in VR, with implications for designing text input spaces that balance movement comfort and writing performance.

X. Cheng, Y. Zhang, Y. Wei, R. Jiang, P. Hansen, H.N. Liang

This work investigates how available writing space affects bare-hand logographic character handwriting in VR. By comparing handwriting performance under different spatial constraints, the study provides design implications for VR text-entry interfaces that must balance recognition accuracy, arm movement, comfort, and the physical limits of immersive environments.

TVCG / IEEE VR 2026

Reorienting with the Bare Hand: Gesture-Based Techniques for Orientation-Enabled Teleportation in Virtual Reality Compares bare-hand gestures for specifying orientation during VR teleportation, asking how users can move and reorient without relying on controllers.

Y. Wei, X. Cheng, R. Shi, H.N. Liang

This paper studies controller-free orientation-enabled teleportation in VR. It compares bare-hand gesture techniques for specifying both destination and facing direction, showing how gesture design can support more fluid, expressive locomotion while reducing reliance on handheld devices.

CSCW 2026 - Poster - Accepted

Private Non-Visual Addressivity Cues for Blind and Low Vision Users in Mixed-Ability Social VR Explores how social VR can privately communicate who is being addressed, supporting blind and low vision users without making access cues awkwardly public.

Z. Zhang, Y. Zhang, X. Cheng*

This poster explores addressivity in mixed-ability social VR: how blind and low vision users can privately know when speech, gaze, or interaction is directed toward them. The work proposes non-visual cue designs that support social participation without forcing accessibility information into an awkwardly public channel.

ECSCW 2026 - Poster

Negative Danmaku as Social Warning Cues in Video Credibility Assessment Looks at whether negative danmaku comments can function as lightweight social warning cues when viewers judge the credibility of video content.

X. Cheng, Y. Zhang, Y. Qiao, P. Hansen, R. Jiang

This study examines whether negative danmaku can act as social warning cues during video credibility assessment. Rather than treating comments as noise, the work asks how real-time collective reactions may shape viewers' attention, skepticism, and trust judgments when they encounter potentially questionable video content.

AHIMR / ISMAR 2026 - Workshop - Accepted

Toward AI-Mediated Candidate Delivery for Chinese Pinyin Input in Virtual Reality Investigates how AI-mediated candidate delivery may reduce selection burden during Chinese Pinyin input in VR, where attention, hand movement, and text choice all compete.

X. Cheng, Y. Wei, H.N. Liang

This workshop paper explores Chinese Pinyin input in VR, focusing on the burden of candidate selection when users must divide attention across text, hand movement, and immersive context. It frames AI-mediated candidate delivery as a way to make VR text entry feel less interruptive and more compatible with embodied interaction.

Under Review

XR-Read: Eye-Level Clarity Matters for Sustained Reading in Head-Mounted Displays Examines how text position and eye-level clarity affect sustained reading in head-mounted displays, turning a subtle display issue into a measurable reading experience problem.

J. Wang, X. Cheng, B. Hou, H.N. Liang

XR-Read studies sustained reading in head-mounted displays, where text clarity can vary with eye level and visual position. The work investigates how these display conditions influence reading experience over time, offering evidence for designing XR reading interfaces that are not merely legible at a glance but comfortable for longer sessions.

UbiComp / ISWC 2026 - Demo Short Paper - Accepted

Demonstrating FlowWalk: AR Guided Outdoor Mindful Walking with Photo Capture Based Nature Awareness Demonstrates FlowWalk as an AR smart-glasses experience that combines intention input, route recommendation, breathing cues, nature capture, and reflective summaries for outdoor mindful walking.

Z. Li, K. Huang, H. Tang, X. Cheng, H. Li, S. Zhao, P. An

This demo presents FlowWalk as an end-to-end AR smart-glasses prototype for outdoor mindful walking. The system combines walking intention input, route recommendation, spatial guidance, breathing prompts, nature photo capture, and post-walk reflection to support a lightweight, situated form of mental well-being practice.

UbiComp / ISWC 2026 - Poster - Accepted

FlowWalk: AR Guided Outdoor Mindful Walking with Photo Capture Based Nature Awareness Presents the FlowWalk poster version, focusing on the system design and study framing for AR-guided outdoor mindful walking with photo-capture-based nature awareness.

Z. Li, K. Huang, X. Cheng, H. Tang, H. Li, S. Zhao, P. An

This poster introduces the design rationale behind FlowWalk, an AR-guided outdoor walking system that encourages nature awareness through route guidance and photo capture. It positions outdoor movement, mindful attention, and wearable interaction as a combined design space for everyday well-being support.

HCII 2025

AI-Echo: Exploring Technological Alienation Through an Interactive Art Experience Uses an interactive art experience to make technological alienation visible, inviting participants to encounter AI mediation as something felt rather than only explained.

Y. Zhang, X. Cheng, J. Yan

AI-Echo is an interactive art experience that explores technological alienation through AI-mediated self-representation. By transforming participants' visual presence and social encounter with the system, the work turns an abstract concern about AI and identity into an embodied, perceptual, and emotionally legible experience.

The Application of Serious Games in Inclusive Education Reviews how serious games can support inclusive education by connecting playful interaction, multimodal access, and learning participation.

R. Jiang, S. Zhang, Y. Zhao, X. Cheng

This book chapter reviews the application of serious games in inclusive education, discussing how game-based systems can connect multimodal access, motivation, learning participation, and AI-supported adaptation.
