TVCG / ISMAR 2026 - Accepted

Effects of Spatial Constraint on Bare-Hand Logographic Character Handwriting Performance in Virtual Reality A study of what happens when handwriting leaves the desk: how invisible, virtual, and physical writing spaces reshape Chinese character input in VR.

X. Cheng, Y. Zhang, Y. Wei, R. Jiang, P. Hansen, H.N. Liang

Abstract. Spatial constraints significantly influence motor performance in virtual reality (VR), as interactions often involve hands hovering in mid-air. Yet their specific impact on logographic handwriting, such as Chinese and similar characters, remains underexplored. While VR text entry research is predominantly alphabetic-centric, especially English, handwriting support for complex scripts is fragmented across systems that treat physical or virtual guidance as implementation details rather than analytical or design variables. To address this, we compare three spatial constraint forms under a unified input pipeline: Unconstrained Mid-Air, Virtual Collision Surfaces, and Anchored Physical Surfaces. In a 3 x 3 within-subjects study (N = 24) with users writing Chinese characters of various complexity, we identified a critical stability-efficiency trade-off. While constrained conditions reduced trajectory length and rewrite counts, they increased normalized writing and total input time compared to mid-air input. Subjectively, U-MAH was rated as the most usable. Compared with HVS, HAVS reduced physical and temporal demand while still producing shorter trajectories and fewer rewrites than U-MAH. We also contribute design implications on when freer mid-air input may be preferable and when planar or physically supported writing may be more beneficial in VR handwriting.

TVCG / IEEE VR 2026

Reorienting with the Bare Hand: Gesture-Based Techniques for Orientation-Enabled Teleportation in Virtual Reality Turns VR teleportation into a bare-hand choreography problem: where to go, which way to face, and how much control a gesture can comfortably carry.

Y. Wei, X. Cheng, R. Shi, H.N. Liang

Abstract. Teleportation is a widely adopted locomotion technique in virtual reality (VR), with reorientation commonly integrated into commercial systems to reduce physical turning and improve navigational control. However, research on reorientation-enabled teleportation has received limited attention and still lacks clear design guidance, resulting in longer interaction times, greater complexity, and higher mental workload. To bridge this gap, we revisit reorientation-enabled teleportation through bare-hand interaction, a natural and device-free modality that is now increasingly supported and widely adopted in mainstream VR systems. Accordingly, four gesture-based techniques, Concurrent Wrist Rotation (C-WR), Decoupled Wrist Rotation (D-WR), Joystick Wrist Rotation (J-WR), and Direction Drawing (DD), were introduced and evaluated in a controlled user study with 24 participants. Results show that C-WR was fastest but error-prone, D-WR improved accuracy at the cost of time, J-WR supported precise orientation but was demanding and slow, while DD offered balanced performance and was most preferred overall. From these findings, four design implications were distilled, emphasizing efficiency, intuitive mappings, and support for diverse user preferences. Finally, two example extensions illustrate how these techniques may extend to broader VR applications.

CSCW 2026 - Poster - Accepted

Private Non-Visual Addressivity Cues for Blind and Low Vision Users in Mixed-Ability Social VR Asks a small but socially loaded question in VR: how can someone know they are being addressed without making accessibility itself publicly visible?

Z. Zhang, Y. Zhang, X. Cheng*

Abstract. Social virtual reality often relies on visual avatar behaviors such as head orientation, spatial arrangement, and turn-taking cues, creating barriers for blind and low vision users in small-group interaction. This project explores private non-visual addressivity cues that help BLV users perceive when attention is directed toward them without publicly marking assistance needs. Through formative co-design with BLV participants and VR designers, we identified breakdowns around speaker attention, turn readiness, and being acknowledged. We then implemented orientation-based cues delivered through either haptic feedback or spatial audio and evaluated them in an exploratory within-subject study with 11 BLV participants. Both cue conditions improved perceived access to conversational attention relative to baseline. Audio cues appeared useful for conversational flow and synchrony, while haptic cues more consistently supported perceived inclusion and social presence. These findings suggest that accessible social VR should treat modality, privacy, density, and user control as core design dimensions.

ECSCW 2026 - Poster

Negative Danmaku as Social Warning Cues in Video Credibility Assessment Treats danmaku not as background chatter, but as a fast-moving social signal that can redirect suspicion, trust, and attention while watching video.

X. Cheng, Y. Zhang, Y. Qiao, P. Hansen, R. Jiang

Abstract. Danmaku (real-time bullet comments overlaid on videos) adds a visible social layer to viewing, but may also shape credibility judgments. We report an exploratory mixed-methods study using CogniTrust, a danmaku interface that logs user behavior. Forty-six participants watched 15 short clips containing true and false claims and judged each clip as "Likely True" or "Likely False" under either negative or neutral danmaku. Compared with neutral danmaku, negative danmaku produced faster decisions (M = 13.04 vs. 16.48 s) and more likely false judgments (M = 78.3% vs. 68.1%). Replay behavior was lower but not significantly different. Interview data suggest that participants treated negative danmaku as socially visible warning cues that oriented suspicion and sometimes reduced the felt need to re-check details. We argue that danmaku can mediate credibility assessment by shifting viewers toward faster, more skeptical, and more socially mediated evaluation.

Toward AI-Mediated Candidate Delivery for Chinese Pinyin Input in Virtual Reality Reimagines the humble Pinyin candidate bar as a spatial XR problem: where should language choices appear when eyes, hands, and attention are already busy?

X. Cheng, Y. Wei, H.N. Liang

Abstract. Chinese Pinyin input introduces a repeated candidate-selection step that is largely absent from alphabetic VR text-entry research. This short paper reframes Pinyin candidate selection in head-mounted VR as a spatial information-delivery problem: after entering a pronunciation, users must repeatedly inspect, reach for, and confirm candidates that occupy visual and motor space. We report a lightweight formative probe with six experienced Pinyin users using a fixed VR candidate bar. The probe was not intended to evaluate an AI-mediated interface; instead, it used the fixed bar to identify where AI-mediated presentation may help. Participants could complete the task, but candidate access still introduced visual shifts, mid-air selection effort, and interruptions to input flow. They welcomed lightweight support such as highlighting or access adjustment, while worrying that automatic layout changes could reduce predictability. We derive four requirements for AI-mediated candidate delivery: preserve spatial predictability, translate candidate likelihood into lower visual or motor access cost, intervene only when context suggests need, and keep final text commitment under explicit user control.

XR-Read: Eye-Level Clarity Matters for Sustained Reading in Head-Mounted Displays Looks beyond â€œis the text readable?â€?toward a sharper XR question: when does visual clarity become the difference between sustained reading and sickness?

J. Wang, X. Cheng, B. Hou, H.N. Liang

Abstract. Extended reality (XR) is evolving into a general-purpose computing platform, yet its adoption for productivity is hindered by visual fatigue and simulator sickness. While these symptoms are often attributed to latency or motion conflicts, the precise impact of textual clarity on physiological comfort remains undefined. Here we show that sub-optimal effective resolution, the clarity that reaches the eye after the full display-optics-rendering pipeline, is a primary driver of simulator sickness during reading tasks in both virtual reality and video see-through environments. By systematically manipulating end-to-end effective resolution on a unified logMAR scale, we measured reading psychophysics and sickness symptoms in a controlled within-subjects study. We find that reading performance and user comfort degrade exponentially as resolution drops below 0 logMAR (normal visual acuity). Notably, our results reveal 0 logMAR as a key physiological tipping point: resolutions better than this threshold yield naked-eye-level performance with minimal sickness, whereas poorer resolutions trigger rapid, non-linear increases in nausea and oculomotor strain. These findings suggest that the cognitive and perceptual effort required to resolve blurry text directly compromises user comfort, establishing human-eye resolution as a critical baseline for the design of future ergonomic XR systems.

UbiComp / ISWC 2026 - Poster - Accepted

FlowWalk: AR Guided Outdoor Mindful Walking with Photo Capture Based Nature Awareness Positions mindful walking as an interaction design problem: how can AR help people notice the world without turning the walk into another screen task?

Z. Li, K. Huang, X. Cheng, H. Tang, H. Li, S. Zhao, P. An

Abstract. As a beneficial contemplative practice, outdoor mindful walking can support mindfulness and connection with nature, but beginners often struggle to stay engaged and connect inner experience with nature. Effectively connecting with nature typically requires support, and can be difficult without therapist guidance. Research shows that connecting with nature can support mindfulness and affect regulation, yet few studies have examined how interactive systems can scaffold this connection during outdoor mindful walking. We present FlowWalk, a mixed reality system that combines lightweight AR cues and mindfulness audio guidance with photo capturing based nature awareness, which generates brief mindfulness guidance from users' captured natural elements. In an evaluation involving 12 users, results show improvements in state mindfulness, self-compassion, mood regulation, and connectedness to nature. Photo capturing based nature awareness enhanced nature noticing and helped participants connect their inner feelings.

UbiComp / ISWC 2026 - Demo Short Paper - Accepted

Demonstrating FlowWalk: AR Guided Outdoor Mindful Walking with Photo Capture Based Nature Awareness A wearable AR walk that nudges attention outward to nature and inward to feeling, using routes, breath cues, photo capture, and reflection as gentle scaffolds.

Z. Li, K. Huang, H. Tang, X. Cheng, H. Li, S. Zhao, P. An

Abstract. Outdoor mindful walking can support mindfulness and connection with nature, yet beginners often struggle to remain engaged and to relate their inner experience to the surrounding natural environment. We present FlowWalk, a mixed reality system that combines lightweight AR cues and mindful audio guidance with photo capturing based nature awareness, and also recommends outdoor mindful walking routes based on users' current location and selected time. Photo capturing based nature awareness generates brief mindfulness guidance from the natural elements users photograph. In an evaluation with 12 participants, results showed improvements in state mindfulness, self-compassion, mood regulation, and connectedness to nature. Photo capturing based nature awareness further supported nature noticing and helped participants link inner feelings with what they observed in nature.

HCII 2025

AI-Echo: Exploring Technological Alienation Through an Interactive Art Experience An interactive artwork about meeting oneself through an algorithmic mirror, where AI mediation becomes something felt, performed, and socially negotiated.

Y. Zhang, X. Cheng, J. Yan

Abstract. Digital technology has greatly facilitated people's daily lives, but it has also brought forth problems like self-objectification and a sense of emptiness. One of the main issues is the alienation caused by technology. We evaluate the connection between human-computer interaction, art, and communication, focusing on a pilot study of the interactive art installation AI-Echo. The study provides a novel viewpoint on how individuals use technology, examining how it affects the way people present themselves and interact with others. We also share the findings, insights, and lessons we've learned from observing how people behave and express their emotions in environments governed by algorithms.

Game Industry Blue Book - 2025

The Application of Serious Games in Inclusive Education Connects serious games, AI, and inclusive education as a design space for motivation, multimodal access, and more participatory learning experiences.

R. Jiang, S. Zhang, Y. Zhao, X. Cheng

Abstract. This chapter reviews the application of serious games in inclusive education, focusing on how game-based systems can support learners with diverse abilities, needs, motivations, and access conditions. It discusses serious games as a bridge between educational goals, playful interaction, multimodal perception, and adaptive technologies, including AI-supported learning experiences. By connecting application scenarios, aesthetic motivation, multimodal access, and learning participation, the chapter frames serious games as a promising approach for making educational experiences more engaging, inclusive, and socially accessible.
